Theory & Key Rules

1

Spot the common factor

Find the highest common factor (the HCF, or the biggest number that divides exactly into every term) and take it outside a bracket.

$$6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3)$$
2

Factorising quadratics

A "quadratic" just means an expression with an x² term in it.

In x² + bx + c, "b" is the number in front of x (called its coefficient) and "c" is the plain number on its own (called the constant).

To factorise, find two numbers that multiply together to give c and add together to give b.

$$x^2 + bx + c = (x + p)(x + q)$$
3

Difference of two squares

A "difference of two squares" is one square number (or square term) with another one subtracted from it, like a² - b².

Take out a common factor first if there is one, then it always splits into two matching brackets — one with a minus, one with a plus.

$$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$$

Lesson Worked Examples

1. Find the HCF of 12 and 18 (the biggest number that divides exactly into both, with nothing left over): $$\text{HCF} = 6$$

2. Divide each term by 6: $$12x \div 6 = 2x, \quad 18 \div 6 = 3$$

$$\text{Final Result: } 6(2x - 3)$$

1. Find two numbers that multiply to -15 and add to 2: $$5 \times (-3) = -15, \quad 5 + (-3) = 2$$

2. Write as two brackets: $$(x + 5)(x - 3)$$

$$\text{Final Result: } (x + 5)(x - 3)$$

1. Take out the common factor 5 first: $$20x^2 - 5 = 5(4x^2 - 1)$$

2. Spot the difference of two squares (since $$4x^2 = (2x)^2$$ and $$1 = 1^2$$):

$$4x^2 - 1 = (2x - 1)(2x + 1)$$

$$\text{Final Result: } 5(2x - 1)(2x + 1)$$
